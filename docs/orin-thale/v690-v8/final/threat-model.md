# Orin Thale v690-v8 bounded threat model

## Assets

The protected assets are the parentless owner history, immutable planning/x1/x2 anchors, proposal definitions, retained failures, exact manifests, private-route boundary, global skill and public-runner provenance, and the distinction between repository preparation and task delivery.

## Threats and controls

1. **History contamination.** A predecessor commit could be made an accidental ancestor. Control: the planning root is parentless, the Caelen/Avelin/Sable hashes are provenance fields only, and direct-parent checks bind the three descendants.
2. **Lifecycle leakage.** X2 evidence could appear in x1. Control: x1 staged review permits only the x1 prefix, x1 truth records x2 absence, and x2 began only after pushed four-way equality.
3. **Failure erasure.** A recovered parser, projection, invalid subject or scanner self-hit could be relabelled as a pass. Control: every failed witness has its own negative identifier and zero original-subject credit; recovery has a distinct passing witness.
4. **Outcome promotion.** Structural success could promote represented, open or exact-gated work. Control: each proposal freezes one disposition, and x2 preserves it even when the typed fixture passes.
5. **Authority laundering.** Consent, appeal, remedy, delegation or accessibility words could be mistaken for real authority. Control: every result carries authority reservations and the claim firewall denies real-world conversion.
6. **Private-route disclosure.** A task identifier, callable route, transcript, private path or credential could enter Git. Control: only exact public titles and repository-relative paths are durable; five-class scanning adjudicates bounded owner text.
7. **Global collision.** A promotion could overwrite an existing skill or runner. Control: every exact target was proven absent or byte-identical before writes; non-identical collisions stop the helper.
8. **Runner drift.** Published runners could differ from reviewed candidates. Control: candidate and installed SHA-256 hashes match, and each installed runner was self-tested after publication.
9. **Canonical replay.** A success could be run twice. Control: the external invocation latch binds exact head, attempt count and receipt; success forbids replay.
10. **Successor duplication.** Orin could receive a duplicate activation or a substitute task could be contacted. Control: live roster refresh, active-plus-archived exact-title resolution where supported, immediate reread, duplicate guard, and one accepted-send stop.

## Residual risk

The scanner, tests and same-owner review can be incomplete. External task service state can drift. Global skill consumers can misuse instructions. Real social, legal, cultural, safety and identity effects remain untested. These residuals preserve the open and exact gates and the `NOT_READY_FOR_STAGE_20` verdict.
