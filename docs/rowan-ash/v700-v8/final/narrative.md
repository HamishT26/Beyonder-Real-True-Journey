## 4. Why the certificates check one another

The core inequalities can be written as lower and upper bounds on individual coordinates. Let the grand value be T. For player i, the singleton lower bound is L_i = v({i}), and the complementary-pair constraint gives U_i = T - v(N minus {i}). The three-player core is the intersection of the box L_i <= x_i <= U_i with the plane x_0 + x_1 + x_2 = T.

That box-plane intersection is nonempty precisely when each lower bound does not exceed its upper bound and the total T lies between the sum of the lower bounds and the sum of the upper bounds. These five conditions provide a direct comparator to explicit coalition-boundary vertex enumeration. The code checks both the existence decision and the full deduplicated vertex set. Lower-dimensional points and line segments remain valid results rather than being discarded as numerical degeneracies.

Relaxation changes the bounds to L_i minus epsilon and U_i plus epsilon. The smallest feasible epsilon is therefore the maximum of five rational quantities: the three half-gaps (L_i minus U_i)/2, the lower-sum gap (sum L_i minus T)/3, and the upper-sum gap (T minus sum U_i)/3. This finite derivation is checked against basis enumeration in the three-dimensional coordinates consisting of two allocation variables and epsilon.

Balanced collections give the dual view. Positive weights cover each player exactly once. For three players, the enumerator recovers five minimal supports: the singleton partition, the three singleton/complement partitions, and the three-pair collection with one-half weights. Their normalized excess bounds match the five direct epsilon conditions. The engine retains a maximizing balanced certificate and feasible primal vertices. Exact equality of those bounds establishes optimality in the declared finite problem without rounding.

The terminology and general core/balancedness connection follow established research, including [On balanced sets and cores](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/nav.3800140404) and the definitions in [On the set of balanced games](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14341v1). The historical paper's metadata and references were accessible; its full text was not retrieved.
