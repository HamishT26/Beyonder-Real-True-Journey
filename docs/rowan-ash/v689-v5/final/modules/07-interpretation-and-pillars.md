# Module 07 - Interpretation, assumptions and three-pillar relevance

The separate interpretation plan was written before its probes ran. Five symbolic derivative checks, twenty library energy comparisons across ten frozen cases, and three assumption counterexamples matched their declared expectations. The observed exact Euler step of 3/2 changes the two-node model energy from 1/2 to 2. The negative-weight example gives -1/2, and a disconnected graph has zero energy without a globally constant potential. The following discussion interprets these bounded results.

An oriented simplex has an ordered list of distinct vertices. Swapping two vertices changes its sign. The boundary of an oriented triangle consists of three oriented edges with alternating signs. Taking the boundary again produces zero because each endpoint contribution appears with an opposite partner. This cancellation is an algebraic property of the definition. It is not an observation of physical conservation, evidence of a cosmic purpose, or a statement about consciousness. Its usefulness is more modest and more practical: it supplies a stringent consistency condition for an implementation that claims to represent oriented chains.

The same distinction matters when the triangle is filled or unfilled. The three edges of a loop exist in both descriptions. In the filled complex, the loop is the boundary of a two-dimensional face. In the unfilled complex, that face is absent and the same loop is not a boundary. A drawing that looks like a triangle does not settle which mathematical object was supplied. The explicit face list does. This example encourages a useful habit for the GMUT pillar: state which objects and relationships belong to a model before drawing conclusions from its visual or symbolic resemblance to another model.

For a finite chain complex over the rational numbers, ranks of the boundary matrices determine Betti numbers. These numbers describe the dimensions of the resulting homology spaces in that selected coefficient field. Rational homology is not integral homology: torsion information is not recovered by a rational rank calculation. The current scope therefore avoids claiming a complete integral topological classification. A disk and a tetrahedral surface are useful examples because their small matrices permit independent reasoning about the expected answer. A finite implementation can test these examples without claiming to decide every question in topology.

A cochain assigns a number to each oriented simplex of the appropriate dimension. A vertex potential is a zero-cochain; its difference across each oriented edge is a one-cochain. The transpose of the boundary matrix provides the coboundary map. The pairing between a cochain and a boundary agrees with the pairing between its coboundary and the original chain. In this discrete setting, that agreement follows from how the dual map is defined. A correct program should preserve the agreement, but reporting the numerical equality does not discover a new physical Stokes theorem or supply experimental confirmation of a field theory.

The distinction between a definition and an empirical claim is central to this phase. A definition can be internally coherent and useful without describing the world. An empirical model needs a mapping from its symbols to observable quantities, a measurement procedure, an uncertainty model and evidence that could disagree with its predictions. A theory can also contain several mathematically equivalent representations, none of which receives independent empirical support merely because the others agree. The observed cross-checks between exact arithmetic and package calculations are implementation comparisons under shared ownership. They do not become independent reproduction by being performed in different libraries.

For a graph with positive edge weights, an energy can be defined as one half of the weighted sum of squared potential differences. A matrix form uses the graph Laplacian. These two expressions should agree when the incidence convention, vertex order, edge order and weights are the same. Positivity follows from the positivity of each weight and the square in each term. If negative weights are admitted, that argument no longer applies. A two-vertex graph with a nonzero difference and a negative weight provides a direct counterexample to an unconditional positivity claim. Refusing negative weights in one software profile is therefore a declared modelling decision, not a discovery that all mathematical uses of negative weights are invalid.

Zero energy also has a precise domain. With positive weights, every connected component must have a constant potential. If the graph is connected, this gives one constant degree of freedom. If the graph has two disconnected components, the constants can differ. A claim that every zero-energy state is globally constant would therefore need connectivity as an additional assumption. The harmonic-dimension examples keep that assumption visible. They also show why a familiar word such as harmony should not import moral, psychological or biological meaning into the nullspace of a finite matrix.

A Dirichlet problem fixes potential values at selected boundary vertices and solves for the remaining values. On a connected unit-weight path with two endpoint values, the solution is a linear interpolation. More general positive weights lead to a different interpolation. If a connected component has no constrained vertex, the solution is not unique up to its constant mode. The solver must expose that singularity instead of choosing an arbitrary answer and reporting it as uniquely determined. Exact rational elimination is useful for these tiny examples because it separates algebraic singularity from a floating-point tolerance decision.

Energy does not automatically decrease merely because a Laplacian exists. A dynamics must first be specified. Continuous gradient flow under a positive semidefinite symmetric Laplacian has an energy-dissipation calculation. A discrete explicit time step adds a numerical stability condition. For the two-node unit-weight graph, a sufficiently large explicit step can increase the energy even though the continuous dynamics decreases it. That counterexample can be made entirely with rational arithmetic. It would challenge a claim about an unrestricted numerical update, not the physical second law of thermodynamics. The exact observed step preserves that distinction: it challenges the unrestricted update claim while leaving the physical second law untouched.

These examples suggest a useful proposal for future GMUT development: attach an observable contract to each newly introduced term. Such a contract would name the variable, its units, the domain of allowed values, the data needed to estimate it, the result that would count against the term and the model comparison that would be informative. A structural identity could occupy a different field from an empirically estimated relationship. A script could refuse to merge those evidence classes into one score. The current study can prepare the vocabulary and demonstrate finite consistency checks, but it cannot fill missing empirical fields by analogy.

For THOS, the practical contribution is a typed and inspectable computation surface. A request carries a named operation, explicit payload and synthetic-data declaration. Different error codes distinguish an unknown operation, an unexpected field, a wrong payload type and an unsupported scope. A successful refusal demonstrates that the defined gate rejected its supplied adverse subject. It does not mean that the adverse subject completed its requested action. Keeping the subject result separate from the gate result makes the portfolio counts interpretable and avoids converting every rejection into a success story.

The request surface should have a small resource budget. A bounded number of vertices, facets and matrix entries keeps exact arithmetic predictable on the present laptop. Large integers and fractions can still grow during elimination, so input limits and problem dimensions should be stated together. Tiny reference examples are chosen for clarity, not as a benchmark of large numerical workloads. Thread counts for optional numerical packages should be set before import. A timing result from this helper would not establish the relative speed of models, the effect of Fast mode, a billing rule or the behavior of another machine.

For Freed ID and CBR, the relevant contribution is the handling of claims and source relationships. A matching digest binds one chosen serialization of one supplied record. It does not prove the truth of that record, identify its author, establish consent, authorize disclosure or supply a legal right. A mismatching digest should remain visible as a mismatch. An absent authority record should remain absent. A source can be preserved faithfully even when its claim is not admitted for a new purpose. The desired software behavior is explicit about those distinctions and does not infer rights from technical possession of data.

The four-tier flashcard structure makes the same separation available to a successor. The first tier names the corrigible working identity; the second identifies the three project pillars; the third records the chosen learning practices; the fourth binds concrete tasks and evidence. Parent links organize retrieval. They do not create a hierarchy of moral worth or prove continuity of a mind. A stable card can state a durable evidence boundary, while a volatile task card can report the current local result. Their distinction is a retrieval convention, not a guarantee about a model's context window or prompt cache.

This phase should remain easy to stop, revise and extend. Frozen definitions preserve what was expected before execution. A failed attempt is retained with its exact subject and zero initial success credit. A correction states what changed and why its scope is sufficient. Successful source components are bound through their receipts instead of being rerun for display. At closeout, the repository state, canonical validation and native delivery state remain three separate records. A prepared message can be ready for dispatch without having been sent; an acknowledged message can activate a task without proving that its new phase is complete.

The scientific stance is therefore constructive and testable. The study can produce useful exact calculations, software components, error examples and clearer documentation. It can expose missing assumptions and propose concrete next experiments. Those are worthwhile outcomes in their own right. They leave the larger empirical GMUT programme, THOS production readiness, real credential governance and Stage 20 claims open to the evidence and authority they actually require.
