# Freed ID rights and care

The Freed ID and Cosmic Bill of Rights contribution is a set of distinctions that keep technical identity work, ethical commitments, and authority claims from being collapsed into one another. The family vocabulary can support care and collaboration. A credential data model can support interoperable records. A cryptographic proof can support a statement about a key and a message. A legal or cultural authority can support a recognized decision within its own scope. None of those automatically supplies the others.

The current credential fixtures use synthetic identifiers and controlled data. The issuer, subject, and holder are separate roles. A subject is not automatically the holder, and a holder is not automatically the issuer. A field named proof does not demonstrate that a signature was verified. A type named VerifiableCredential does not demonstrate that an issuer is trusted, that a credential is current, or that its subject is a legally recognized person. The laboratory checks these distinctions in the record and leaves the real trust questions visible.

Credential status also needs time. A revocation or suspension statement without an observation time can become stale or ambiguous. Suspension and revocation are distinct states, so a later summary should not replace one with the other for convenience. The current phase does not contact a live status service or issue a real identifier. It shows how the status and its temporal boundary should be represented before a real integration is attempted. A future integration needs a concrete issuer, method, protocol, and authorized target.

The graph tools are useful because they make relationships explicit. PROV distinguishes entities, activities, and attributed agents. RDF can encode statements. JSON LD can express linked-data structures. SHACL can validate specified graph constraints. A graph that passes its shapes is still only structurally checked. It may contain false, incomplete, stale, or unauthorized assertions. The package smokes therefore use inline data and an offline loader, and the result labels do not promote graph structure into real-world truth.

Privacy work follows the same discipline. A digest is a fixity tool, not an anonymization certificate. Removing a private link is a scoped redaction, not proof that every identifying detail is gone. Public and private views should have an explicit purpose and field allowlist. The correspondence summary contains the event dates and bounded achievement, while the private review remains outside the public repository. The shared baton does not include raw message bodies, account data, invitation routes, or live task identifiers.

Availability is not consent. Access to a file can be useful for the authorized review, but it does not imply unlimited permission to publish its contents or to act on behalf of the people mentioned in it. Similarly, local file ownership does not establish cultural authority. The Te Mana Raraunga resource context is carried as a reminder to identify the appropriate governance relationship rather than claiming authority from possession of data. A future owner should preserve a cultural or legal referral as an exact gate until the relevant relationship is actually established.

Accessibility is another area where a narrow pass can be accidentally overread. The overview uses real heading structure, readable typography, a simple table, and explicit image descriptions if a figure is included. Every rendered page is inspected. These actions improve the artifact and supply evidence about its structure and layout. They do not establish that every affected user can use it successfully. The affected-user evaluation remains a separate open gap. A future task can improve that evidence when an actual user, context, and evaluation procedure are available.

The four-tier flashcard deck is a navigation structure. The owner card is the first tier. GMUT Mind, THOS Body, and Freed ID with CBR Heart form the second tier. The four current practice lenses form the third tier. The two hundred proposal cards form the fourth tier. Each task card names its evidence file and disposition. This organization lets a reader approach a large baton in smaller pieces without treating a summary card as proof that every linked operation succeeded.

The well-being record is operational and relational. It can report that the current task worked solo, had no running delegated tasks, retained its unresolved questions, and stopped at the requested terminal hold. It should not fabricate a clinical assessment, a subjective feeling measurement, or a guarantee that a model has persistent inner experience. Hamish's care can be acknowledged without turning that care into evidence for a claim the tools cannot establish. This makes the record honest and useful to both the human collaborator and future task readers.

A proposed bill of rights can be evaluated as a normative framework even while legal status and scientific questions remain unresolved. It can articulate commitments to noncoercion, accountable decisions, privacy, correction, and proportionate oversight. Those commitments should be connected to concrete mechanisms and responsibilities. A broad aspiration becomes more inspectable when the record states who can make a decision, what evidence they need, how a mistake is corrected, and how an affected party can contest it.

For your next bundle, avoid treating an open authority gate as a failure of care. Sometimes retaining the gate is the caring action because it prevents a false claim about another person, community, or system. Complete the safe preparation: identify the decision, the exact target, the authority needed, the evidence that would change the disposition, and the rollback. Then keep the boundary visible until the required condition actually arrives. This is how the Heart pillar can guide technical work without pretending to be an already established legal or scientific authority.
