5. Counterexamples and interpretation

The codebook `0, 01` is a useful positive example. It is not prefix-free because `0` is a prefix of `01`, yet it is uniquely decipherable. This shows why rejecting every non-prefix codebook would conflate a convenient sufficient condition with the actual property under study. The result does not imply that all non-prefix codebooks are suitable for streaming or that their decoding delay is acceptable in a real application. Delay and operational recovery remain separate questions.

The codebook `0, 01, 10` gives a concrete negative example. The bit string `010` has the two parses `0 | 10` and `01 | 0`. Its Kraft sum is exactly one, so satisfying the Kraft inequality is not sufficient for unique decipherability of the given words. Similarly, `0, 00` has two parses of `00`. The reports preserve both index streams rather than choosing one as if it were authoritative. For each fixture, the viewer and JSON model expose the complete reachable graph, the uniqueness decision and the ambiguity witness when one is found.

The length-series coefficients count sequences of codeword indices having a given encoded length. When a codebook is ambiguous, different index sequences can encode to the same binary string. Therefore these coefficients are not described as counts of distinct binary strings. Reversal and binary-symbol permutation are also treated as transformations of the exact declared codebook, with both before and after decisions retained. They do not supply real channel measurements or production performance results.
