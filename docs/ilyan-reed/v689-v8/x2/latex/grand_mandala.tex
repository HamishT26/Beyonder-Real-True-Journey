This phase-local research candidate is not empirical confirmation or canon.
Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$, $\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3$, and $c=\hbar=1$.
\begin{align}
S&=\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M^2}{2}F(\phi)R-
\frac12 Z(\phi)\nabla_\rho\phi\nabla^\rho\phi-V(\phi)\right]+S_m,\\
M^2 F G_{\mu\nu}&=T^{m}_{\mu\nu}+T^{\phi}_{\mu\nu}+M^2D_{\mu\nu},\\
T^\phi_{\mu\nu}&=Z\left(\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi-
\tfrac12g_{\mu\nu}(\nabla\phi)^2\right)-Vg_{\mu\nu},\\
D_{\mu\nu}&=\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu F-g_{\mu\nu}\Box F,\\
0&=Z\Box\phi+\tfrac12 Z'(\nabla\phi)^2+\tfrac{M^2}{2}F'R-V'.
\end{align}
For $F>0$, $\kappa=M^{-2}$ and nonzero constant dimensionless $\alpha$,
\begin{align}
G_{\mu\nu}&=\kappa T^m_{\mu\nu}+\alpha\Omega_{\mu\nu},\\
\alpha\Omega_{\mu\nu}&=\kappa(F^{-1}-1)T^m_{\mu\nu}
+\kappa F^{-1}T^\phi_{\mu\nu}+F^{-1}D_{\mu\nu}.
\end{align}
This defines an effective remainder; it does not independently identify $\alpha$.
On a coupled solution with conserved minimally coupled matter,
$\nabla^\mu(\alpha\Omega_{\mu\nu})=0$ is required by the Bianchi identity.
The arbitrary flat-space proposal $\Omega_{00}=t$, other components zero, has
$\partial^\mu\Omega_{\mu0}=-1$ and fails that condition.
For $F\equiv1$, constant $\phi$ at a stationary potential, and $V=M^2\Lambda$,
$G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=\kappa T^m_{\mu\nu}$.
Observables, likelihood, calibration, uncertainty, competing baseline and a
falsifier remain required. No physical data or fitted coefficient is supplied.
