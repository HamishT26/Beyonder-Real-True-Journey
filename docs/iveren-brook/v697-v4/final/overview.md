Several limitations are constructive parts of the contract. A stationary point can be a saddle or maximum. A positive semidefinite Hessian does not guarantee that Hx=-g is consistent. A zero Hessian can leave a nonconstant linear objective. A chosen representative of a stationary family does not describe all feasible members of that family. Likewise, the finite box candidate list witnesses a minimum value without enumerating every point in a continuous minimizer set. The code's unique-candidate flag is therefore kept distinct from a uniqueness theorem about the optimizer.

Boundary signs make the convex certificate understandable. A free interior coordinate has zero gradient; at a lower bound the gradient must be nonnegative; at an upper bound it must be nonpositive. A fixed coordinate has no feasible displacement, so a nonzero gradient there is not an automatic violation. Feasibility and positive semidefiniteness are separate requirements. An affine pullback uses nonzero diagonal scales and shifts; negative scales reverse the corresponding bounds. These explicit cases make it possible to criticize a result without inventing a physical interpretation.
