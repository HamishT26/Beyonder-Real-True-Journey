## 04. Mathematical domain and explicit limits

The new domain is the exact two-variable quadratic f(x)=one-half x-transpose H x plus g-transpose x plus c over a declared closed box. H is a symmetric rational two-by-two matrix. Bounds may coincide, which makes fixed-coordinate semantics necessary. Input numerators and nonzero denominators are signed, bounded integers; outputs reduce fractions with positive denominators. Requests have closed fields, exact vector dimensions, bounded wire size and depth, and reject duplicate decoded keys, trailing data, nonfinite values, malformed fractions, nonsymmetric matrices and reversed bounds. Requests cannot choose filesystem paths, commands or real records.

Stationary equations are Hx=-g. The selected representative sets free coordinates to zero under left-to-right pivots, while retaining the homogeneous nullspace. Inconsistent equations remain inconsistent. Curvature uses determinant and trace. A stationary saddle is not a minimum; a semidefinite matrix can coexist with inconsistent stationary equations. Zero Hessian with a nonzero linear term is not a constant objective.

Box candidates comprise corners, admissible nonflat-edge stationary points and the chosen full stationary representative when feasible. They witness the minimum value in this bounded domain. They do not enumerate a continuous minimizer set. A unique listed candidate does not prove a unique optimizer. Convex certificates require feasibility, positive semidefiniteness and the appropriate gradient signs: zero inside, nonnegative at lower bounds, nonpositive at upper bounds, with fixed coordinates handled separately. Parameter choices are assumptions. No exact arithmetic operation supplies measured coefficients, physical robustness, professional allocation authority or empirical GMUT confirmation.
