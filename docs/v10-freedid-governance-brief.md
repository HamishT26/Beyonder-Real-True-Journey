# V10 Freed ID Governance Brief

## Evidence posture
- confirmed_evidence: repo-backed DID, recourse, and governance validation remains authoritative.
- inference: current standards/governance anchors refine wording and next-proof tasks.
- open_gap: legal force and real-world enforcement claims remain bounded.

## Current standards anchors
- [W3C DID Core](https://www.w3.org/TR/did-core/): canonical DID syntax, document structure, verification relationships, and resolution model.
- [W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/): current VC packaging, claims model, and verification flow.
- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework): risk-governed lifecycle expectations and controls language.
- [OECD AI Principles](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles): current high-level governance principles for trustworthy AI.
- [UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence): human-rights-centered ethical baseline.
- [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights): rights baseline for dignity, recourse, and participation claims.
- [EU AI Act official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng): current regulatory anchor for risk-tiered AI obligations.

## Comparative posture
- confirmed_evidence: Freed ID remains strongest where repo-backed certificate integrity, signature checks, recourse pathways, and bounded authority surfaces are already validated locally.
- inference: DID Core and VC 2.0 support clearer wording around identity documents, verification methods, and credential semantics, but they do not by themselves prove legal legitimacy or production governance readiness.
- open_gap: stronger proof is still needed for external legal recognition, interoperable issuer/verifier adoption, and independent governance enforcement outside the repo-local proof boundary.

## Next proof tasks
- refresh alignment, gap, and next-proof columns against the current W3C, NIST, OECD, UNESCO, UDHR, and EU anchors.
- preserve the repo-first governance record and keep local proof separate from aspirational legal or civilizational claims.
- prioritize the next externalizable proof set around DID document interoperability, credential schema portability, and explicit recourse test cases.
