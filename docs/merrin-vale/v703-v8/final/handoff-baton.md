## 4. What global consistency means here

A satisfying assignment is one complete assignment that obeys every declared relation. A nonempty natural join therefore proves finite satisfiability. Global consistency is stronger: projecting the complete join back onto every relation's scope must reproduce that original relation exactly. Every original local tuple must extend to a complete solution. Pairwise consistency only compares projections across relation intersections, so it can miss an obstruction involving a larger cycle. These are separate predicates and remain separate fields in the implementation and viewer.

The odd-cycle fixture demonstrates pairwise agreement without any complete assignment: its recorded consistency contract has zero solutions and six unsupported local tuples. The final nonempty inconsistent-cycle fixture has one solution while four original tuples still lack a global completion. Thus finding one solution does not certify all local claims. Conversely, an all-empty relation family can satisfy projection equality while remaining unsatisfiable. This empty-case distinction is deliberately preserved. The theoretical context is the Boolean relation discussion in Atserias and Kolaitis, Consistency, Acyclicity, and Positive Semirings (2020). Only the stated finite Boolean specialization was implemented; generalized semiring or physical claims do not follow.
