Registered review and preregistration layer
v469 should also introduce research discipline from Open Science practices.
The Center for Open Science explains that preregistration helps separate exploratory research from confirmatory research; confirmatory work rigorously tests a predicted effect, while exploratory work searches for possible relationships or unexpected discoveries.
That gives the Journey a strong rule:
Exploratory work may discover.

Registered Reports go even further: COS describes them as a format where the research question and methodology are reviewed before data collection, and high-quality protocols can receive provisional acceptance if the authors follow through with the registered method.
For GHC, we translate that into:
Do not change success criteria after seeing results.
Do not hide failed or negative experiments.
Do not call exploratory results confirmatory.

Preregistration and registered-review discipline
The Center for Open Science describes preregistration as a way to distinguish exploratory from confirmatory research: confirmatory work tests a predicted effect, while exploratory work searches for possible relationships and unexpected results.
v469 should make this a GHC law:
Exploratory work can discover.
Confirmatory work must define the test before the result.


The Center for Open Science describes Registered Reports as a publishing model where research questions and methodology are reviewed before data collection, and high-quality protocols can receive provisional acceptance before results are known.
GHC translation:
Do not move the goalposts after the result.
Do not hide negative tests.
Do not call exploration confirmation.


Apply this to:
GMUT physics tests
Ψ-field simulations
GHC eval benchmarks

Albion Mandala prototype tests
