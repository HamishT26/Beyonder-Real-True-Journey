# Evidence-led progress for the ChatGPT × Dots phase programme

**Documentation update — 22 November 2026**

This note describes a proposed evidence and coordination method for the next thirty-phase programme. Its purpose is to make research, engineering and creative work easier to inspect across tools and environments. It also records a limited implementation milestone: an offline ledger prototype and a collection of original synthetic models. This documentation update does not publish their source code, deploy services, or claim that the complete framework has been implemented in this repository.

## Count distinct things distinctly

A large programme needs several measures of progress. Projects, tasks, tests, documents, research hypotheses, simulations and messages are different units. Adding them together produces an impressive number with an unclear meaning. The ledger therefore keeps typed counts and reports each type separately.

Every counted unit receives a stable identifier and a work key. An artifact supporting two pillars can carry both tags while remaining one artifact. Repeating a test increases execution attempts, while the count of unique test cases stays unchanged. A corrected document remains part of its version lineage. Changing its filename or moving it between projects should not create an additional accomplishment.

A project is a bounded question or deliverable with acceptance conditions. Its component tasks may be complete while the project remains open. Conversely, a completed research project may conclude that a hypothesis fails. Completion describes whether the agreed investigation was performed; it does not require a favorable finding.

## Keep evidence states explicit

The proposed workflow distinguishes several claims that are often compressed into the word “done.”

| Evidence state | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Proposed | A bounded scope and acceptance condition have been written. |
| Ready | Required inputs and prerequisites are available. |
| Running | A specified execution has actually begun. |
| Observed | An output or effect has been recorded. |
| Producer verified | The producer checked the output against stated acceptance conditions. |
| Independently reviewed | Another reviewer examined the identified evidence. |
| Independently reproduced | A separate execution reproduced the relevant result under a documented method. |

These states are not interchangeable. A documentary review can be completed without inventing a runtime. A test needs an actual executed case. An independent source review does not become an independent reproduction simply because it was performed by a different participant.

Evidence is bound to its exact subject. A successful project-level observation cannot automatically promote every task or document attached to that project. Executed evidence should identify source, case, runtime and output. An independence declaration should identify the producer evidence being checked and the method used.

An append-only event history preserves corrections and transitions. Hash chaining can expose changed events when compared with a trusted retained reference. It does not authenticate identities or protect against an adversary replacing both the complete history and its expected reference. Those remain separate design requirements.

## Treat unknown effects as unresolved state

A timeout does not establish that an operation failed without effect. A command may have completed after its caller stopped receiving responses. Repeating it immediately can duplicate a message, reservation or mutation.

The proposed unknown-effect guard prevents automatic promotion or retry until the earlier operation has been reconciled. Reconciliation records the observed outcome, the evidence used, and the scope of what is now known. If the observation is insufficient, the item remains unresolved.

This rule supports persistence without blind repetition. A failed read can be investigated with an alternative read method. An uncertain write needs evidence about the destination before another write. Retry counts should measure attempts to resolve a concrete uncertainty, not reward repeated actions that leave that uncertainty unchanged.

## What the development build actually established

The latest inspected ledger build report records **21 unique current test methods, all passing**, with no current failures, errors or skipped methods. Across earlier and current runs it records **59 test-method invocations**. Those are separate numbers: repeated attempts do not become additional unique tests.

The tested prototype includes typed counts, evidence binding, transition checks, duplicate detection and the unknown-effect reconciliation guard. Tests also exercise preservation of existing ledger bytes after rejected operations, output escaping and rejection of a missing ledger as a successful empty result. The scope is an offline, single-file prototype with synthetic fixtures. It does not execute remote commands, install packages, authenticate distributed participants, or operate a live messaging service.

The accompanying portfolio contains **30 proposed projects and zero recorded project executions**. The ledger implementation is a real engineering output. It is not evidence that the thirty proposed projects have already run. Earlier unsuccessful attempts remain part of the development record rather than being hidden by the final passing suite.

## Illustrations, simulations and empirical findings

The model workbench contains **15 original synthetic assets**, divided equally among physical/mathematical literacy, system concepts, and identity/rights concepts. **Six include executed numerical or algorithmic runs**; the other nine are static mathematical constructions or conceptual graphs.

The executed examples cover an oscillator, conservative lattice diffusion, a bounded queue, resource reservation, checkpoint replay and a simple civic allocation rule. For example, the queue accounts for 90 arrivals as 57 completions, 15 rejections and 18 remaining tasks. The diffusion run preserves normalized mass to an absolute error below \(3\times10^{-16}\).

These results establish properties of their stated toy inputs and algorithms. They do not establish a new field of nature, validate GMUT, measure production infrastructure, create legal rights, or demonstrate consciousness. A graph arranged in three coordinates remains a conceptual graph when its coordinates have no physical meaning.

The generator passed 25 invariant assertions. A separate verifier passed 16 saved-data and syntax checks. A clean rebuild produced 22 identical assets. Browser visual review was blocked before page load, so rendered accessibility, mobile presentation and visual correctness remain unverified.

The following primary repositories provide concrete material for evaluating future integrations. Their inclusion here is a reference proposal, not a claim that they have been installed, combined, audited or deployed.

Each integration should pin a version, document its license and dependencies, define the required capability, and test a small acceptance fixture. Repository availability is an input to evaluation. It is not evidence of compatibility with every platform or of successful integration.

## Next acceptance conditions

The next engineering milestone is a reproducible execution on a clearly identified environment, followed by review of the resulting evidence. It should demonstrate that a proposal cannot become an executed success without the required history, that an unknown effect cannot silently become a retry, and that the same typed work item cannot be counted twice.

The next visualization milestone is an actual desktop and mobile browser review, including keyboard navigation and text equivalents. The next research milestone is a specified hypothesis with a measurable prediction, a comparator, and an outcome that could reject the hypothesis.

Future reports should publish the narrow result, its prerequisites, its unresolved limits and the evidence supporting its status. That practice lets the programme grow while keeping every claim small enough to examine.
